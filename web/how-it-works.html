Memoir

How Memoir works

Only the time you spend using your computer counts.

Memoir times the app in front of you, and stops when you step away. Here’s exactly what counts, when the clock stops, and what’s kept.

What counts

  • The app you’re using, in the window that’s in front. Apps behind it don’t count, even if a video is playing in them.
  • Websites, under their name, like youtube.com, in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, Arc and Comet. Memoir reads the site’s name from the browser’s address bar, or from Memoir in that browser once you link them; until it can, the time counts under the browser.
  • A browser is an app too, with all its time, and the websites in it count as well: they’re the same minutes, seen two ways. Most used shows them as two lists, Apps and Browsers. Your totals, your limit, your categories and your hours count each minute once.
  • Memoir itself, as Memoir. The desktop and the taskbar don’t count.
  • By the hour. Each day keeps how long you spent in each app and on each site, and in which hours, so the report can show your day hour by hour.

When the clock stops

  • You haven’t touched the mouse or keyboard for 60 seconds. If the app in front is playing sound, like a video or music, it keeps counting.
  • The screen locks, or the computer goes to sleep.
  • You pause it with Track screen time in Settings, or Pause in Memoir’s menu in the notification area.
  • You quit Memoir. Closing or minimising its window doesn’t stop it: it keeps counting from the notification area.

Starting with Windows

  • Memoir starts when you sign in to Windows, straight into the notification area, without opening its window, so a restart doesn’t leave a gap in your day.
  • Turn off Start with Windows in Settings, or Memoir in Windows’ own Startup apps, and it starts only when you open it. Either switch turns it back on.
  • If tracking is paused, Memoir still starts, but paused: nothing counts until you resume it, from Settings, Today or its menu in the notification area.

The clock in the notification area

  • Click it for Today, right beside it: your day so far, the same view Memoir shows from your browser’s toolbar. A click anywhere else, or Esc, closes it.
  • Right-click it for Memoir’s menu: open Memoir, Today, pause or resume tracking, or quit.
  • Double-click it to open Memoir. Resting the mouse on it shows how much time you’ve spent today.

Private time

Apps and websites on your Never track list still count towards your total and your limit, but only as one Private line, with no names. Adding one makes its past time Private too. Private (Incognito) browser windows count as Private only with Memoir linked in that browser, or the optional browser connector, allowed to run in them; without either, Memoir can’t tell a private window from any other.

What’s kept

  • Each app’s and site’s name and its time, hour by hour, and each app’s icon. Never what you type, click or see.
  • Page and window titles, only if you turn on Page titles in Settings: up to 30 an app or site each day, the rest as “other pages”.
  • Everything is kept for as long as you choose under Keep history (forever, unless you pick a shorter time).
  • It all stays on this computer: in the Data folder next to Memoir, and in the browsers you link. More in the privacy policy.

Linked with Memoir in your browser

  • Optional. Add Memoir to Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, Arc or another browser built on Chromium, and press Link in its Settings, under Your data: that browser and Memoir for Windows show the same week. You choose to merge both histories, or to start from today.
  • Both keep counting. Each keeps the other’s days apart from its own and shows the two merged: where both counted the same hour, the larger time counts, never the two added together. A browser and its sites are the same minutes too: they count once.
  • Every browser you use. Link each one: Memoir passes each one’s time on to the others, and its Settings show a row for each.
  • One set of rules. Never track, Page titles, Keep history, the weekly reminder, your daily limit, categories, pausing and Appearance (the theme, the cursor and the Rainbow clock) follow whichever side changed them last.
  • While Memoir for Windows is open, it sends the limit and weekly notifications, so each comes once.
  • Unlink from either side and both keep what they have. While linked, Clear all on either side clears both.
  • It runs through your browser’s native messaging and a named pipe only your Windows account can open: on this computer, never online.

Your daily limit

  • The ring is green, then yellow at 75% of your limit, orange at 90% and red at 95%, when you get a notification. Past the limit it turns deep red with a flame, and you’re notified once more.
  • Once you commit to a limit, it’s locked: it stays the same on the days after. Only Settings can undo it, after a run of questions.

Why it may differ from other screen-time tools

Windows and other screen-time tools often count any time the screen is on. Memoir counts only while you’re using your computer, or while the app in front plays sound. So its numbers are usually smaller, and they tell you where your time went.

The clock is real

Memoir’s clock tells the time: the dot goes round the ring once a minute, the long hand shows the minutes and the short hand the hours, on your computer’s own time. Like the one at the top of this page.
